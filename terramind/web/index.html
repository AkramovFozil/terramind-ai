    How TerraMind works

    1. Reference sites on Mars (Gale crater floor — Curiosity; Meridiani Planum — Opportunity) and the Moon (Mons Mouton Plateau — an Artemis III candidate region).
    2. Every site gets a 25 × 25 km window in its own local azimuthal equidistant projection: Mars sphere R = 3,396,190 m, Moon sphere R = 1,737,400 m, Earth WGS84.
    3. All DEMs are brought to one 463 m grid. Three metrics: slope (Horn), local relief (elevation − window median), roughness (TRI).
    4. Each metric's distribution is compared with histogram intersection on fixed bins; the overall score is a weighted mean (weight sets A and B are both reported).
    5. A person reviews every window's quick-look and approves it before any score exists. Changing a window afterwards blocks scoring.
    6. The Analog Finder slides the same window across a region of Earth (NASA SRTM) and ranks every position. Water and nodata are excluded. Leads go to human review.
    7. The AI only explains the computed numbers and can trigger a search; a grounding check flags any number not in the results.

    Limits

    • Shape only, at 463 m: nothing about rock type, minerals, climate, radiation or habitability.
    • One window per site; another window can score differently.
    • MOLA near the equator is partly interpolated between orbit tracks; SRTM dates from February 2000.
    • Meridiani was added after the first score; this and every other decision is logged in the repository.

    Data

    • Mars: MGS MOLA DEM 463 m — USGS Astrogeology / NASA PDS.
    • Moon: LRO LOLA south pole DEM 80 m — NASA GSFC PGDA (Barker et al. 2023).
    • Earth: NASA SRTM GL1 / GL3 v003 — NASA LP DAAC via OpenTopography.
    • Map imagery: Esri World Imagery, OpenStreetMap, NASA GIBS Blue Marble — for human review only, not scored.

    AI use

    Explanations are generated by a large language model through OpenRouter. Code was written with AI coding assistants. Scores, rankings and maps are computed by TerraMind's open-source algorithm.
